01 — The river

Every mark is a commit

The picture above is a river of 3,176 dots. Each dot is one real commit, taken from the git history of Atlas, the academy portal and Quartz, my browser. The dots flow into a timeline running from 1 March to 12 October 2026, so a mark sits on the day its commit landed and a tall plume is a day with a lot of them. The three currents are the three repositories: Atlas, the portal, then Quartz. The 34 projects, their dates and their states are written out as a table under “The register”, below.

Atlas · 2,720 Academy portal · 210 Quartz · 246 Bucketed by UTC author date · 136 days with a commit

I build software and put it to work. Atlas, my AI task automation service, has been live at try-atlas.app since 27 August. A youth hockey academy runs attendance, coaching notes and its roster on a portal I built over the summer. Every day I use Quartz, a browser I wrote that opens Claude sessions, desktop apps and my phone as tabs. Since 21 September it has run on Mantle, my own layer over the Windows 11 desktop, and since 7 October I run Claude Code from Helm, an app of mine on the desktop and my phone.

How to read the river

  • Left to right is the calendar: 1 March to 12 October 2026.
  • Three currents, one per repository. A dot is one commit; a tall plume is a day with a lot of them — the deepest is 5 September, 208 commits to Atlas.
  • The width of a current is that repository’s share of the work: Atlas 2,720 commits, the portal 210, Quartz 246.
  • Point at the water for the day under the pointer and its count, and run the pointer through it to move it. The 34 projects are listed with their dates and states in the register below.

06 — The numbers

875,541 lines, counted once

875,541 code lines across 3,364 files, measured on 12 October 2026. Atlas is the biggest share, about a quarter, and roughly three fifths of Atlas is tests. Mantle, my Windows 11 desktop, is next.

Two different rules are in play across this site and they do not reconcile, so they are never shown side by side. This census counts code lines. The per-project figures in the write-ups above come from git ls-files | wc -l, which counts every line in a different file set.

About these numbers

Measured on 12 October 2026 from git history, file counts and the live sites, counting commits through 11 October. The commit figures above are Atlas, the portal and Quartz; across all 31 of my repositories it is 3,750. Where a project has no git history, its dates come from its files. Trading results, client and family data, and security details are left out on purpose.

How I work

I build with Claude Code as a pair programmer. A few of the projects here, like Helm, my Claude Code mods, Mission Control and VOLT, are tools I made for working that way, and all four are open source on GitHub.

How this site is made

The sunset at the top is one 2D canvas and no library: the sky and the sea, three headland ranges and the first stars, moved by a camera that pulls back once and then stops. Under the rest of the page is a second fixed canvas — the same bay, the same evening — and there the scroll is the camera's altitude: sea level at the masthead, six hundred kilometres over the water at the foot. The horizon and its curve, how far the coast opens, when the town comes up over the bulge, where a cloud at nine kilometres sits once you are above it: all of it falls out of one sphere and one pinhole projection, not a stack of parallax layers. The clouds drift and a meteor burns on their own slow clock, and only while the page is in front of you; under reduced motion it is still frames, moved by the scroll and nothing else. The river is my own WebGL: one vertex per commit, positions integrated on the CPU and uploaded once a frame. No graphics library, no build step. It stops the moment it settles. More in the colophon.
