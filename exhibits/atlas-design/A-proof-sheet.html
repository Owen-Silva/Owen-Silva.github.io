Atlas grades every run before it reaches you.

This is how these tools fail quietly.

A confident number arrives in the same email, at the same time, looking exactly like a good one. So Atlas grades every run before it reaches you — blank replies, refusals, answers cut off mid-sentence, claims with no source. Each gets its own tag in your run history.

Six verdicts exist. Five of them are ways of failing.

TYPE CASE 05 · SIX DIES · COMPARTMENTS CUT TO HOW OFTEN YOU REACH IN · EACH FLOOR STILL CARRIES THE LAST IMPRESSION

The six verdicts

  • ok

    The answer stands up. Nothing to say about it.

  • empty

    Nothing came back. The email would have been blank.

  • refusal

    The model declined to answer at all.

  • truncated

    Cut off mid-sentence. You would have read half a forecast.

  • loop

    It repeated itself until it ran out of room.

  • [unsourced]

    A claim with nothing behind it. This is the one that reads best and is worth least.

Zapier, Make and n8n do most of this better.

Zapier and Make are visual editors with large connector libraries, and both now ship AI assistance too. Atlas starts from the other end: describe the outcome, AI works out how.

Less setup for open-ended, judgement-heavy tasks — and, honestly, far fewer integrations than they have.
