03The ask

What it watches

If you can say it in a sentence, Atlas can watch it.

Its territory is the things outside your accounts — a forecast, a price, a page, a score. It cannot read your inbox or your connected apps, and it is not pretending to.

04How it works

Three beats

One sentence in. A schedule out of it. A graded answer back.

01

The sentence is the task.

You write what you want and when you want it in the same breath. Atlas splits the sentence itself — one half becomes the schedule, the other half becomes the prompt. There is no second screen.

02

The schedule is a real position on a real axis.

Ten frequencies exist. Four of them are free. Pro adds the six sub-daily ones. Plotted honestly, on log time, that looks like this — and it shows the thing worth admitting: the free floor is daily, which is slower than the free tiers you are comparing this to.

03

The result arrives already graded.

Every run is checked before it is sent, and the verdict is kept with the run. Free gets one digest a day at an hour you pick. Pro gets each run as it lands, and you can reply to refine it.

  • schedule daily 07:00

05The check

The argument

Atlas grades every run before it reaches you.

This is how these tools fail quietly: a confident number arrives in the same email, at the same time, looking exactly like a good one. So Atlas grades every run before it reaches you — blank replies, refusals, answers cut off mid-sentence, claims with no source. Each gets its own tag in your run history.

  • okComplete, and it carries a source.
  • emptyThe model returned nothing at all.
  • refusalThe model declined to answer.
  • truncatedCut off mid-sentence.
  • loopIt repeated itself instead of finishing.
  • unsourcedA claim arrived with nothing behind it.
05cont.

It is not a warning email and it is not a retry loop. The task stops, and the next thing you hear about it is a notice you can clear in one click.

You hear about it once a week at most — one click snoozes it for thirty days, and the first good run clears the flag on its own. Nothing here nags.

07Versus

Against the incumbents

Zapier, Make and n8n are better at most of this.

They are visual editors with very large connector libraries, and both of the big two now ship AI assistance as well. Atlas starts from the other end: describe the outcome, and the model works out how — which means less setup for open-ended, judgement-heavy work, and, honestly, far fewer integrations than they have.

Use them

If the automation is a fixed chain between specific apps — this record, into that sheet, then that message — a visual editor is the right tool and it is not close. Their free tiers also run every 15 minutes. Atlas's free floor is daily.

Use Atlas

If the automation is "watch this and tell me what matters," where the work is reading and judging rather than moving fields between two apps. One sentence, a schedule, and a grader that will stop the task rather than keep sending.
