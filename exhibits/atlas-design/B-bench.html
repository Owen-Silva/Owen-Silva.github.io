05The check

The part nobody else does

A bad answer arrives looking exactly like a good one.

This is how these tools fail quietly: a confident number arrives in the same email, at the same time, looking exactly like a good one. So Atlas grades every run before it reaches you — blank replies, refusals, answers cut off mid-sentence, claims with no source. Each gets its own tag in your run history.

You hear about it once a week at most — one click snoozes it for thirty days, and the first good run clears the flag on its own. Nothing here nags.

And then it stops on its own

Three consecutive flagged runs and the task pauses itself, so a question that has quietly stopped working does not keep spending your quota or your attention.

The six tags are the ones the code actually writes, not six chosen for a marketing page: ok, empty, refusal, truncated, loop, unsourced. Six, and only one of them is good news.

07Comparison

Against Zapier, Make and n8n

Two shapes of automation. Only one of them is Atlas.

Zapier and Make are visual editors with large connector libraries, and both now ship AI assistance too. Atlas starts from the other end: describe the outcome, and the model works out how.

ATLAS · NEXT TO A CHAIN

TRIGGER FILTER FORMAT ACTION ACTION

A FIXED CHAIN BETWEEN SPECIFIC APPS · THEY ARE BETTER AT THIS

Every morning, tell me the forecast for Boston — rain, temperature, and whether I need a coat.

WATCH THIS AND TELL ME WHAT MATTERS · THIS IS ATLAS

Less setup for open-ended, judgement-heavy tasks — and, honestly, far fewer integrations than they have. If your automation is a fixed chain between specific apps, they are the better fit and you should use them.

Some other things that are true and not flattering: plain English input is not unique to Atlas — Zapier ships Copilot free. Their free tiers run every fifteen minutes; the Atlas free floor is daily. Forty runs a month is the smallest free allowance in the category. There is no public API, no mobile app and no SSO.

08Pricing

Two prices
